## Abstract

Define one typed scalar predicate and fact intermediate representation (IR) for static verification.
The IR describes scalar terms, comparison atoms, Boolean formulas, canonical normal forms, a
three-valued decision procedure, and a constant evaluator that matches runtime semantics. It also
defines the fact context, stable-path dependencies, reachability, the boundary between proven and
trusted facts, and the versioned library-index encoding used by separate compilation.

Type and null facts remain in the existing flow layer. The fact context combines those facts with
scalar formulas at its boundary; the scalar IR does not embed type descriptors. The IR is the shared
foundation for checked newtype construction, comparison flow facts, predicate functions, interval
and difference-bound reasoning, range loop facts, and parameter refinements. It does not add a
general refinement-type system.

## Motivation

The static-verification roadmap proposes several visible features, but they need the same six
operations:

- normalize `x < n`, `n > x`, and `not (x >= n)` to a common form;
- substitute arguments or a newtype carrier into a predicate;
- decide whether a predicate is true, false, or unknown;
- combine facts across branches and loops;
- invalidate facts when a binding or stable path is written; and
- export a predicate through a compiled library.

Implementing those operations separately in each feature would produce incompatible normal forms,
inconsistent constant semantics, and duplicated diagnostics.

The current compiler has no shared predicate IR. `ConstantFolder` is a bytecode constant folder: it
has no substitution environment, no fact context, no serialization, and no stable-path model. The
compiled-library index records only a `validated` boolean for a guarded newtype and reconstructs the
validator as `true`, so the predicate itself is not available to consumers. Both are adequate for
their current uses and inadequate for static verification.

A foundation proposal keeps later proposals focused on their own semantics instead of re-litigating
comparison normalization and serialization in each one.

## Goals

- Define a typed term, atom, and formula IR for scalar predicates.
- Keep type and null facts in the existing flow layer and combine them with scalar formulas at the
  fact-context boundary.
- Define canonical normalization so equivalent forms compare reliably.
- Provide a three-valued decision procedure: `TRUE`, `FALSE`, or `UNKNOWN`.
- Define reachable and unreachable contexts so vacuous truth cannot suppress diagnostics.
- Provide implication and subsumption queries for discharging refinements.
- Define a constant evaluator whose results match runtime semantics exactly.
- Define a fact context with assume, query, join, substitution, and invalidation operations.
- Reuse the stable-path rules from [ZEP-0131](./ZEP-0131.md) rather than inventing a second
  stability model.
- Separate facts that are proven by analysis from declarations that are trusted.
- Define a closed, versioned encoding for exported scalar predicates and refinement signatures.
- Define the diagnostics vocabulary for proved-false, unprovable, and unsupported predicates.

## Non-goals

- A general SMT solver, arbitrary theorem proving, or complete predicate decidability.
- A purity, effect, or ownership analysis for arbitrary expressions.
- Type or null facts inside the scalar formula; they stay in the existing flow layer.
- A general implication engine in the core solver; interval reasoning belongs to
  [ZEP-0142](./ZEP-0142.md).
- General refinement types, dependent types, or liquid types.
- Flow facts over mutable fields, array slots, call results, or custom getters.
- `Float` interval reasoning or NaN-sensitive flow facts in the first version.
- Changing declared types, runtime representations, or JVM descriptors.
- Promising optimization or bounds-check elimination from a successful proof.

## Proposal

### Layers

The IR has three layers:

```text
Term    ::= Const(value : ScalarType)
          | Path(stablePath)
          | Carrier(path)       // newtype representation
          | Length(path)        // array or string length
          | Add(Term, Term)
          | Sub(Term, Term)
          | Neg(Term)

Atom    ::= Compare(operator, Term, Term)   // <, <=, ==
          | NamedPredicate(predicate, [Term])

Formula ::= True | False
          | Atom
          | And(Formula, Formula)
          | Or(Formula, Formula)
          | Not(Formula)
```

`>` and `>=` are surface spellings oriented into `<` and `<=`; `!=` is surface syntax normalized
to `Not(==)`. None of the three appears in the canonical IR. Type and null facts are not atoms in
this formula; the fact context combines them with scalar formulas as a separate fact kind.
`NamedPredicate` is filled in by [ZEP-0141](./ZEP-0141.md). The core solver may treat an opaque
named predicate conservatively.

Terms are typed with a closed `ScalarType`: `Int`, `Long`, `Boolean`, or `String`. Initial ordered
comparisons apply to `Int` and `Long`; equality applies to `Int`, `Long`, `Boolean`, `String`, and
guarded newtypes over those carriers through their `Carrier` view. `Float` ground constant
evaluation is allowed for checked construction, but `Float` terms do not enter flow facts or
exported predicates because NaN and signed zero need an explicit model.

### Terms and stability

A `Path` follows the stable-path definition in [ZEP-0131](./ZEP-0131.md): a local, parameter, or
`this` root; read-only storage-backed member segments; no mutable field, mutable property, array slot,
call result, or custom getter; and no reassignment of the root between the fact and its use.

`Carrier(path)` reads the representation of a newtype. It is a zero-cost view at the source level
because concrete newtype positions lower to their representation.

`Length(path)` is an immutable structural observation for arrays and strings. Arrays are
fixed-length and `append` returns a new array; strings are immutable. The length of a stable array or
string reference therefore cannot change during a call. Element reads are not stable and never
appear in a term.

Arithmetic terms are admitted only when the compiler can show that the operation cannot overflow.
`length - 1` is safe for a non-negative length; `x + 1` is not safe for an arbitrary `Int`. When
overflow cannot be excluded, the term is not formed and the fact becomes `UNKNOWN`. The core IR
owns the `Add`, `Sub`, and `Neg` term forms so predicate bodies and exported refinements can express
relations such as `index + 1 <= limit`; the interval backend owns the bounds and overflow reasoning
that decide whether those terms are usable. Section [Overflow](./ZEP-0142.md#overflow) in
[ZEP-0142](./ZEP-0142.md) defines that reasoning. Formation is an admission check, not a solver
query: the core normalizer can canonicalize a formed term, but it does not reason about arithmetic.
`Mul`, `Div`, and `Mod` are outside the term language.

### Atoms, formulas, and normalization

Comparison normalization rewrites orientable operators:

```text
a > b      -> b < a
a >= b     -> b <= a
a != b     -> Not(a == b)
not (a < b)  -> b <= a        // total order
not (a <= b) -> b < a
```

For integer and other total orders, these rewrites are exact. They are not applied to `Float`.
Only `<`, `<=`, and `==` are primitive comparison atoms; a normalized formula never contains `>`,
`>=`, or `!=`.

Formula normalization:

- flatten nested `And` and `Or`;
- sort operands by a stable structural key;
- remove duplicate operands;
- apply De Morgan's laws to reach negation normal form;
- fold ground atoms;
- simplify `And(x, True)` to `x`, `And(x, False)` to `False`, and their duals; and
- drop `True` operands from conjunctions and `False` operands from disjunctions.

Normalization must be deterministic. Equal normalized formulas must compare equal without source
spans, and the same formula must serialize identically across compiler runs. The canonical order is
a total structural order over constructor tags, scalar types, canonical path strings, and constant
values, with deterministic tie-breaking. It must not depend on insertion order, hash iteration, or
object identity. Source spans live in a side table for diagnostics and never participate in formula
equality or encoding.

### Decision procedure

The public query surface is:

```text
decide(context, formula) -> TRUE | FALSE | UNKNOWN
entails(context, premise, conclusion) -> TRUE | FALSE | UNKNOWN
isUnreachable(context) -> Boolean
```

`decide` returns `TRUE` only when the formula is valid under the context. It returns `FALSE` only
when the negation is valid. It returns `UNKNOWN` otherwise. The implementation may be incomplete,
but it must never return `TRUE` or `FALSE` without a sound argument. On an unreachable context it
returns `UNKNOWN`; callers that report diagnostics must check `isUnreachable` first.

`entails` is strong three-valued entailment under the premise: `TRUE` means every model of the
context and premise satisfies the conclusion; `FALSE` means every such model satisfies the negation;
`UNKNOWN` means neither is proved. It is equivalent to `decide(context.assume(premise), conclusion)`
and uses the same conservative rules. An `UNKNOWN` result means the caller must not discharge the
obligation.

The default solver chain is:

1. if the context is unreachable, return `UNKNOWN`;
2. structural lookup in the normalized context, including exact negated atoms;
3. constant evaluation when the formula is ground;
4. unit propagation over Boolean structure and equality union-find substitution;
5. the interval and difference-bound backend from [ZEP-0142](./ZEP-0142.md), when enabled; and
6. `UNKNOWN`.

The core solver deliberately does not attempt general implication, case splitting, or resolution.
It proves only what structural lookup, constant folding, equality substitution, and Boolean
normalization establish; a conjunction of comparison atoms that needs relational reasoning is
delegated to the interval backend. This keeps the default solver predictable and bounded.

### Constant evaluation

Constant evaluation is the bridge between static verification and runtime behavior. It supports:

- literals;
- unary `+`, `-`, `!`, `not`, and `~`;
- integer `Long` and `Int` arithmetic, bitwise operations, and shifts;
- comparisons and Boolean `and`, `or`, and `not`;
- string equality, length, and concatenation when the runtime semantics are exact; and
- guarded newtype carriers when the value is already constant.

It does not support calls, constructors, allocation, array indexing, mutable reads, or arbitrary
member access. A foldable validator that calls a helper is therefore not evaluated at compile time;
the checked constructor falls back to a runtime check instead of rejecting the program.

Semantics must match runtime evaluation:

- `Int` and `Long` arithmetic wraps on overflow;
- division or remainder by zero yields `UNKNOWN`, not a compiler error;
- `Float` comparison and equality follow the language's documented NaN and signed-zero rules;
- `String` equality is the language's string equality, not JVM identity; and
- short-circuit evaluation is preserved.

`Float` constant evaluation is limited to ground expressions whose runtime semantics are already
documented. It does not create `Float` terms for flow facts or exported predicates. The same
evaluator serves `new`, `tryNew`, and predicate checking; there is no separate
checked-constructor evaluator that could drift from runtime behavior.

A differential test corpus must evaluate the same expression at compile time and at runtime and
assert equal results. A mismatch is a compiler bug.

### Fact context

A fact context associates a set of normalized facts with the current program point. It records
binding identities, not source spellings, and it tracks the stable paths mentioned by each fact.
The context holds two fact kinds:

```text
Fact ::= Scalar(Formula)
       | TypeFact(existing flow fact)
```

Only the scalar kind is defined by this proposal. Type and null facts keep their existing flow
representation and are combined with scalar facts at the context boundary. The scalar solver sees
only `Scalar` facts; a query that depends on a type fact is delegated to the flow layer. A
type-intrinsic newtype invariant is a scalar formula derived from the declared type and may be
materialized for a scalar query; it is not stored as a mutable flow fact.

Operations:

- `assume(formula)`: add and normalize a scalar fact; a contradiction marks the context
  unreachable.
- `check(formula)`: run `decide`.
- `join(left, right)`: retain scalar facts present in both contexts; type facts are re-derived from
  the joined environment. The first implementation intersects normalized scalar fact sets;
  entailment-based retention is a later precision improvement. An unreachable branch is ignored. If
  both branches are unreachable, the result is unreachable.
- `substitute(mapping)`: replace paths by new paths or constants for call-site checking.
  Substitution is simultaneous and the result is re-normalized. A substituted term that is not a
  stable path or constant does not establish a fact.
- `invalidate(path)`: remove every scalar fact that mentions the path or any path with the path as
  a prefix. Invalidating `a` therefore removes facts about `a`, `a.b`, `Length(a)`, and
  `Carrier(a)`. A relational fact depends on every term it mentions, so invalidating either side
  removes the fact.
- `killScope(scope)`: discard facts for bindings declared in a leaving lexical scope.
- `isUnreachable()`: report whether the current path is contradictory.

Type-intrinsic facts from guarded newtypes are not stored as mutable flow facts. They are derived
from the declared type at each read, so an assignment to a variable of that type cannot invalidate
them. `join` re-derives them from the joined environment rather than intersecting stored copies.
Flow facts from predicates, comparisons, and parameter refinements are stored and invalidated as
above.

Call invalidation reuses the existing flow engine's assignment, call, loop-backedge, `break`, and
`continue` rules. This proposal does not add a second alias or mutation analysis.

### Trust model

Static verification needs an explicit boundary between what the compiler proves and what it trusts.

| Fact source | Status | Basis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Constant evaluation | Proven | The evaluator implements runtime semantics |
| Comparison formula from a condition | Proven | The condition's true or false path |
| Interval or difference-bound query | Proven | A sound abstract domain |
| Guarded newtype invariant | Trusted by type | Every construction path enforces a stable validator |
| Predicate function body | Checked or opaque | [ZEP-0141](./ZEP-0141.md) restricts checked bodies to the fact IR |
| Parameter refinement | Precondition | Call sites prove it; the callee may keep a runtime backstop |

A guarded newtype's invariant is trusted because guarded newtypes have no unchecked source
constructor. The trust depends on all construction boundaries, including host interop and
deserialization, enforcing validated construction. Host interop and deserialization are therefore an
acceptance gate, not an assumption: before type-intrinsic facts are enabled, the implementation must
show that those boundaries cannot create a guarded value without the validator. If a boundary is not
yet enforced, the affected newtype contributes no type-intrinsic fact. A future guarded newtype over
a mutable representation must define its own stability rule; this proposal only covers the current
immutable `Int`, `Float`, `Boolean`, and `String` representations.

The trust also requires the validator to be a stable predicate over `value` and immutable
observations such as compile-time constants or literal-initialized public immutable scalar or
`String` values. Existing validators may call ordinary functions or read mutable state, and the
language does not promise validator purity. Such a
validator is enforced at construction but cannot be re-evaluated later, so no type-intrinsic fact is
derived from it. Only a validator that fits the fact IR contributes a type-intrinsic fact; the rest
contribute none.

### Serialization and separate compilation

An exported scalar predicate uses a closed, versioned encoding:

- terms refer to positional parameters, `Carrier` or `Length` of a parameter, literal constants, and
  literal-initialized public immutable scalar or `String` values encoded by value;
- no member paths, mutable reads, custom getters, or call results are exported in version 1;
- a public immutable value must have a literal initializer that the exporting compiler can evaluate;
  its qualified name is retained for diagnostics but its value is encoded inline; and
- the normalized formula is encoded, not source text.

The API index gains an optional length-prefixed predicate blob on guarded-newtype exports and on
function and method parameter records. The blob carries its own `predicateEncodingVersion`. A
missing or zero-length blob means no predicate. A consumer that sees an unknown predicate-encoding
version treats the predicate as absent rather than failing the library load. The existing
`validated` boolean remains for compatibility. The outer API-index schema version is bumped once
per release cycle for all new predicate records.

A library that cannot export a predicate omits the blob. Consumers then:

- get no type-intrinsic facts for that newtype;
- cannot statically discharge a corresponding parameter refinement; and
- still remain sound because checked constructors and parameter preconditions keep their runtime
  backstops.

Predicate semantics are part of the public API when exported. Changing a predicate is a behavioral
API change and must follow the artifact and library version rules in
[ZEP-0012](./ZEP-0012.md). The index schema version is bumped once per release cycle; all new
predicate records should share one bump.

### Diagnostics

The feature-specific proposals add the actual catalog entries. This proposal fixes the vocabulary:

- **Provably false**: the predicate is false for the supplied values. This is an error at a
  compile-time-checked site.
- **Not provable**: the predicate may be true, but the current facts do not establish it. The
  diagnostic must say what is missing, not claim the predicate is false.
- **Unsupported**: the predicate uses a construct outside the fact IR. This is a declaration or
  usage error only when the feature requires a checked predicate; otherwise it degrades to no fact.
- **Missing exported predicate**: a cross-library fact is unavailable. This is a note, not an error,
  unless a parameter refinement requires a static proof.

Diagnostics should show the known facts that were considered and offer a relevant quick fix:
introduce a guard, bind a stable local, use `tryNew`, or use a checked entry point. An unreachable
context is not reported as a provably false predicate; diagnostics are suppressed until reachability
is established. Final `ZR####` numbers are assigned at implementation time from the resolution range.

## Compatibility and impact

The proposal is source-additive. It does not change the runtime representation, JVM descriptors, or
existing program meaning. It adds compiler-internal IR, a library-index schema field, and shared
diagnostics.

The work affects the resolver, flow analysis, constant evaluation, library-index production and
loading, and tooling. It does not require `dist/`-visible output changes by itself.

The API-index schema and standard-library API version are separate. This proposal needs a schema
bump when predicate records are exported. The standard-library API version changes only if standard
library predicates or constructors are added and published.

## Alternatives

- **Put type and null facts inside the scalar formula.** This couples the scalar IR to type
  descriptors, generic substitution, and a serializable type identity, and the interval backend
  still cannot use them. Rejected; type facts stay in the existing flow layer.
- **Keep `!=` as a primitive atom.** It duplicates `Not(==)`, makes canonical equality harder, and
  adds an encoding tag with no semantic value. Rejected; `!=` is surface-only.
- **Export member paths by qualified identity in version 1.** It requires stable cross-library
  member identity and a versioning rule for changing public values, while the first feature set only
  needs parameters, literals, and `Length`. Rejected for version 1; a later proposal can extend the
  encoding.
- **Use entailment-based joins in the first implementation.** The query cost and precision policy
  are harder to keep deterministic; intersection is a sound first step. Rejected for version 1.
- **Let each feature define its own fact representation.** This is locally simpler but produces
  incompatible normal forms, duplicate evaluators, and inconsistent separate-compilation behavior.
  Rejected.
- **Reuse `ConstantFolder` as the IR.** It is a compile-time bytecode utility with no substitution,
  context, stability model, or serialization. It can remain the literal-folding backend, but it
  cannot be the shared fact IR. Rejected.
- **Use an SMT solver and a general formula language.** This would reduce `UNKNOWN` results but adds
  a large dependency, non-deterministic behavior, and a harder compatibility story. The roadmap only
  needs a small, predictable, sound subset. Rejected for now.
- **Trust all predicates and skip proof.** This makes `new`, flow facts, and parameter refinements
  unsound in different ways. Rejected.
- **Encode refinements as declared union or singleton types.** This changes the type system and the
  source model much more than the verification goal requires. Rejected.

## Implementation and validation

Likely stages:

1. Add typed terms, atoms, and formulas with deterministic normalization, canonical ordering, and
   structural equality.
2. Add the constant evaluator and a differential corpus against runtime evaluation, including ground
   `Float` behavior.
3. Add fact kinds, the fact context, reachability, stable-path binding, substitution, prefix
   invalidation, and intersection joins.
4. Add `decide`, `entails`, `isUnreachable`, and the structural, constant, equality, and interval
   backends.
5. Add the length-prefixed predicate encoding, its version handling, and API-index round-trip
   support.
6. Wire diagnostics and tooling once the consuming proposals define their source surfaces.

Validation should cover:

- normalization idempotence and determinism, independent of insertion and hash order;
- equivalent spellings such as `x > 0`, `0 < x`, and `not (x <= 0)`;
- constant evaluation on overflow, division by zero, string equality, short-circuiting, and ground
  `Float` NaN and signed-zero cases;
- fact invalidation on assignment, on relational dependencies, and on path prefixes;
- join behavior with facts valid on one path only and with unreachable branches;
- `entails` returning `FALSE` only when the negation is proved, and `UNKNOWN` on unreachable
  contexts;
- serializer round-trips, missing blobs, unknown encoding versions, and missing-predicate
  degradation;
- the host-interop and deserialization validated-construction gate; and
- the full test suite after resolver, flow, and library-index changes.

## Open questions

- Should `Boolean` equality facts be normalized further, such as `x == true` to `x`?
- How should facts interact with `Any` boxing and unboxing?
- Should a later version export public member paths, and if so, how should their identity and
  values be versioned?
- Should `Float` terms enter flow facts once a documented NaN and signed-zero model exists?
- Should the default solver expand named predicate calls transitively in version 1, or leave that
  to [ZEP-0141](./ZEP-0141.md)?

## Decision

Accepted 2026-11-27.

The settled design is:

- the scalar IR has typed `Const`, `Path`, `Carrier`, `Length`, `Add`, `Sub`, and `Neg` terms and
  `Compare(<, <=, ==)` and `NamedPredicate` atoms; `>`, `>=`, and `!=` are surface-only;
- type and null facts stay in the existing flow layer and are combined with scalar facts at the
  fact-context boundary;
- arithmetic term formation requires an overflow-safety proof, while interval reasoning stays in
  [ZEP-0142](./ZEP-0142.md);
- `decide` and `entails` are strong three-valued queries; `isUnreachable` is a separate query and
  unreachable contexts return `UNKNOWN`;
- the first join intersects normalized facts and ignores unreachable branches; invalidation uses a
  path-prefix rule and delegates call invalidation to the existing flow engine;
- exported predicates are closed, length-prefixed, and independently versioned; version 1 exports
  positional parameters, `Carrier(parameter)`, `Length(parameter)`, literals, and literal-initialized
  public immutable scalar or `String` values encoded by value, with no member paths;
- ground `Float` constants use the same evaluator and documented IEEE semantics; `Float` does not
  enter flow facts or exported predicates;
- type-intrinsic facts require host interop and deserialization to preserve validated construction.
