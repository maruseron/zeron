## Abstract

This proposal replaces future version-candidate planning with durable, concern-based workstreams.
A workstream groups related ZEPs into numbered parts that can be designed, implemented, and released
independently. Versions remain release events; they are not containers for unfinished scope. The
central workstream index records the concern-level picture and a release ledger, while the ZEP
tracker remains the source of truth for proposal status and historical shipping.

## Motivation

Version-first planning makes a concern's status depend on whichever future release currently claims
it. When a bundle slips, is reordered, or is dissolved, its ZEP rows must be relabeled even though
the underlying design work has not changed. Mixing different concerns in one future bundle also
makes it difficult to answer durable questions such as "what remains for model metadata?" or "what
depends on scoped receivers?" without reading every proposal.

The `0.10.0` planning state demonstrated the problem: model metadata, resource handling, test
reporting, continuation portability, and static verification were being discussed together under one
tentative version. Several items were not accepted and could not honestly be called candidates, and
the version label implied a commitment the project had not made.

The planning layer should describe concerns and dependencies. The release layer should describe
what actually shipped.

## Goals

- Give each durable area of work a stable identity, mission, and home.
- Split work into numbered parts that can ship independently and span releases.
- Record dependencies and partial completion without assigning future version numbers.
- Keep ZEPs central and permanent, with workstream pages linking to them.
- Preserve released history in a release ledger rather than rewriting it as future plans.
- Give contributors and agents one routing point for current planning.

## Non-goals

- Changing the ZEP proposal lifecycle, numbering, or decision process.
- Changing artifact versioning, release automation, or publication policy.
- Introducing story points, sprints, owners, or a general project-management system.
- Rewriting historical release records to fit the new model.
- Requiring every unscheduled ZEP to belong to a workstream immediately.

## Terminology

- **Workstream** — a durable area of concern with one stable slug and one `README.md`.
- **Part** — an independently scoped, independently shippable slice of a workstream, identified by a
  workstream-local number.
- **ZEP** — the decision record for a design or policy. ZEP numbers remain global and permanent.
- **Release** — a published artifact version. A release may contain parts from several workstreams,
  and a workstream may span several releases.

A workstream is not a release bundle. "Workstream" is used instead of "project" because
`zeron.toml` already uses *project* for a build unit.

## Workstream layout

Each workstream has one folder under `documents/workstreams/` and one `README.md`:

```text
documents/workstreams/
  README.md
  model-metadata/
    README.md
  scoped-resources/
    README.md
```

The central `documents/workstreams/README.md` owns the cross-workstream index and the release
ledger. It does not duplicate per-part status; the workstream page is authoritative for its own
parts, and the central index is a rollup.

No per-workstream tracker file is created. ZEPs remain in `documents/zeps/`; workstream pages link
to them rather than copying their design text.

## Part numbering and status

Parts use stable, workstream-local numbers (`1`, `2`, `3`, ...). Numbers are assigned when the part
is recorded and are never reused or renumbered. A new independently shippable slice receives the
next number. A part may reference one or more ZEPs, but its scope should be independently
reviewable.

Part statuses are:

- **Draft** — the design is open or not accepted.
- **Accepted** — the design is approved; implementation is not complete.
- **Ready** — implementation is complete and dependencies are satisfied; the part is eligible for a
  release.
- **Shipped** — the part is included in a published release.
- **Deferred** — the part is intentionally paused or postponed.

A part may be `Ready` while other parts of the same workstream remain `Draft`. Workstream statuses
are:

- **Proposed** — the concern is recorded but active design work has not started.
- **Active** — at least one part is being designed, implemented, or released.
- **Paused** — no part is currently progressing, but the workstream is not closed.
- **Complete** — every part is shipped or intentionally deferred.

Proposal status in the ZEP tracker and part status in a workstream are different records. A ZEP may
be `Accepted` while its part is still `Accepted`; the part becomes `Ready` only when the code has
landed. A workstream part may be `Draft` even when its ZEPs are not yet written.

## Dependencies and partial shipping

A workstream page records ordering constraints in a `Depends on` column. Dependencies may be within
the workstream or on another concern. A part that is not independently shippable should either be
merged into its prerequisite or explicitly marked as depending on it.

Shipping a subset of parts is normal. A release includes only the parts that are `Ready` and
selected for that release. A workstream remains active until its remaining parts ship or are
deferred; the release event does not close the concern.

## ZEP tracker relationship

The central [ZEP tracker](./ZEP.md) continues to record:

- proposal status (`Draft`, `Accepted`, `Implemented`, `Deferred`, and so on);
- whether a ZEP has shipped and, if so, the published version;
- deferred and not-applicable dispositions.

It no longer assigns future versions. The `Candidate` and `Planned` release states are retired:
they implied a version commitment before release scope was actually approved. A ZEP that belongs to
a workstream remains `Unscheduled` in the tracker until it ships, even when its part is `Accepted` or
`Ready`. Workstream membership is recorded in the workstream pages, not in a new tracker column.
Release-scoped candidates are recorded separately in the active
[release plan](../releases/current.md), not as tracker states; see [ZEP-0179](./ZEP-0179.md).

Historical `Shipped` references remain unchanged. `ZEP-0018`'s final-version convention applies to
those published references.

## Release ledger

The central workstream index includes a release ledger with one row per published release. Each row
records the release version, publication date, the workstream parts included, and a link to the
detailed release history. The ledger is updated when a release is published. Historical releases
that predate the workstream model are recorded as legacy release sets with a link to their ZEP rows.

At release time:

1. Confirm each part selected in the [release plan](../releases/current.md) is `Ready` and its
   dependencies are satisfied.
2. Publish the artifact under the normal versioning policy.
3. Mark the part `Shipped` in the workstream page and record the version.
4. Add or update the release-ledger row and archive the release plan.
5. Mark the corresponding ZEP rows `Shipped` with the published version.
6. Update the central index rollup and the ZEP tracker counts.

## Migration

The initial migration:

- adds the central workstream index and release ledger;
- creates workstream pages for the shipped `0.9.0` concerns and the active/proposed concerns;
- maps the former `0.10.0` planning set and related drafts into workstream parts;
- removes future version labels from planning documents;
- leaves historical release records and proposal decisions intact.

Items outside a current workstream remain ordinary unscheduled ZEPs. They can join a workstream when
a maintainer records a part for them; they do not need a placeholder page.

## Alternatives

- **Keep version bundles with workstream labels.** Rejected because the version label still creates
  false commitments and forces relabeling when scope changes.
- **Use one flat planning document.** Rejected because concern-level status, dependencies, and
  history become difficult to maintain as the number of proposals grows.
- **Create a tracker file per workstream.** Rejected because it duplicates the central index and
  scatters release state. One page per workstream plus one central index is sufficient.
- **Keep `Candidate` and `Planned` for accepted parts.** Rejected because neither implies an approved
  release scope; `Ready` plus release selection is the honest planning state.

## Compatibility and impact

This is a documentation and process change. It does not change compiler behavior, artifacts, or
ZEP numbering. Historical tracker rows and release notes remain valid. Contributors should use the
workstream index for current planning and the ZEP tracker for proposal decisions.

## Validation

- Every workstream page has a mission, part table, statuses, and ZEP links.
- Every part number is unique within its workstream and no future version is assigned.
- The central index rollup matches the part tables.
- The release ledger matches the `Shipped` rows in the ZEP tracker.
- No workstream page or tracker row assigns a future version; release candidates are confined to
  the active release plan.

## Decision

Accepted and implemented on 2026-11-26. Workstreams are the planning layer; versions are release
events. The central workstream index owns the concern rollup and release ledger, the ZEP tracker
continues to own proposal status and historical shipping, and ZEP-0179 adds a separate release plan
for candidate and selected scope.
